Mobility.

The 10-minute pre-lift routine, the neck protocol, and where flexibility work actually happens.

Three separate jobs that get confused with each other: warming up before a lift, protecting the neck, and actually gaining range. Each gets its own slot here.

Pre-lift routine · 10 min, every lift day v1 Changed

Controlled articular rotations and dynamic work — not static stretching. Save the long holds for the sauna.

ClockDrillTimeHow
0:00Standing hip CARs60 sOne leg, brace the core, slow maximal circles at the hip — 1 each direction per side
1:00Hip 90/90 transitions90 sFlow side to side, torso tall, 2 s pause at each end
2:30World's greatest stretch90 sLunge, elbow inside front foot, rotate and reach up, then straighten the front leg for the hamstring — 3 reps/side
4:00Quadruped T-spine rotation90 sAll fours, hand behind head, elbow to opposite hand then open to the ceiling, follow with the eyes — 5–6/side
5:30Shoulder CARs90 sFist, arm forward and overhead, rotate externally, sweep behind and down. Max circle, min speed
7:00Scapular wall slides60 sBack to wall, cactus arms, slide up — stop before the lumbar spine leaves the wall. 8–10 reps
8:00Ankle dorsiflexion Changed60 s — 120 s on squat daysFace the wall, foot 3–4 in away, drive the knee over the toes, heel down. 8–10/side — doubled on squat days
9:00Dead hang60 s2 × 30 s passive. Decompress spine, lats, shoulders. Breathe

The one change: ankle dorsiflexion doubles on squat days, because restricted dorsiflexion is often the real limiter behind restricted knee ROM and squat depth. Squat-day routine runs 11 min rather than 10 — the clock column is the deadlift/press-day version, so on squat days the dead hang starts at 10:00, not 9:00.

Neck protocol · 5 min, every lift day New

This was the biggest gap in v1 — you own a NeckSlevel and use it regularly, but it wasn't written down anywhere, so it wasn't protected when time got short. Five minutes here does more for long-term neck durability than anything else in the program, and it is the first thing to get skipped. Do it on all three lift days, DS or home.

DrillPrescriptionNotes
Chin tucks2 × 10, 5 s hold · ~2 minDeep neck flexors — the muscles that hold your head over your shoulders. Worth doing daily, not just on lift days; they need no equipment
NeckSlevel — alternating direction2 × 10–12 · ~2 minFlexion/extension one session, lateral flexion (both sides) the next. Alternating is what keeps this inside five minutes. Slow and controlled through a comfortable range — never ballistic, never into a pinch
Isometric holds3 directions × 10 s · ~1 minPart of the normal 5 min (2 + 2 + 1) — and the fallback: on any day the neck feels stiff or symptomatic, do only these and skip the other two rows, which makes it a 1-minute day rather than a skipped one
The one rule

Neck training earns its keep through consistency at submaximal effort, not through load. Nothing here should ever feel hard. If a direction produces pain, numbness, or symptoms referring down an arm, that day's neck work stops and it goes to your clinician — that's a nerve-root question, not a training question.

Flexibility — in the sauna New

Flexibility was a stated goal with no dedicated time anywhere in v1. Rather than adding a session, it moves into the sauna you're already sitting in. Tissue is warm, you're not doing anything else, and long holds are the format with the best case for changing actual range — the hold-duration literature is genuinely mixed, but where static stretching does move range, it's longer accumulated time under tension that does it, not a 20-second hold. (Session count: the recovery tracker says four sauna sessions a week; the weekly schedule shows sauna on five days. Four is the number to plan the flexibility dose against — the fifth is a bonus, not a requirement.)

HoldDurationTargets
Couch stretch90–120 s per sideHip flexor and quad — the tissue that pulls the pelvis into the low back. Squeeze the glute and tuck the pelvis under before you lean back, and stop at the first sensation in the low back rather than the hip. Untucked, this drill drives lumbar extension, which is the opposite of what it's here for
Doorway or floor pec stretch90–120 s per sideChest and front shoulder — the direct counter to the pressing volume
Seated or supine hamstring90–120 s per sidePosterior chain; hinge from the hip and keep a neutral spine, don't round into it

Two or three of these per sauna session across four sessions gives each hold roughly three exposures a week — rotate so nothing gets skipped two sessions running. Ninety seconds is the floor: shorter holds reliably change how the stretch feels, and the case that they change range is much weaker. Time cost: 6–12 min per sauna, all of it inside sauna time you already spend. Full modality schedule on the Recovery page.

One caution on doing this in the heat

Long holds at 185 °F, especially standing ones, are a setup for lightheadedness — more so if hydration has been light, or if anything you take affects blood volume or appetite. Get water in beforehand, do the standing pec stretch early in the session rather than at the end, and come out if you feel it. Heat also makes tissue feel more compliant than it is, so let range be governed by sensation, not by how far you can get.

Sunday extended mobility · 19–27 min New

v1 said "extended mobility" on Sundays without saying what it meant, which in practice means it doesn't happen. Here's the session, after the ~15-minute REHIT:

  • Three drills from the pre-lift sequence, unhurried — hip CARs, shoulder CARs and quadruped T-spine rotation, each at half the pre-lift speed, which doubles them from 60 / 90 / 90 s to 2 + 3 + 3 = 8 min. The other five pre-lift drills are deliberately not here: 90/90 and the dead hang get their own longer segments below, and the wall slides, ankle drill and world's greatest stretch are warm-up work with nothing to gain from a slow second pass
  • 90/90 work, 5 min: transitions, then 60-second holds in each end position. This is the pre-lift 90/90 extended from 90 s, not a second helping of it
  • Thoracic openers over a foam roller — extension holds, 4 min. Likely the segment that buys the most: the usual argument is that a stiff mid-back is what pushes the neck and low back into compensating for it, so restoring extension there takes load off both. That is the mechanism, not a measured result. Two rules given your spine: support the back of your head with both hands so the neck never hinges backward over the roller, and keep hips down with ribs pulled in so the movement happens between the shoulder blades and not at the low back. Roller under the mid-back only, never the neck
  • Dead hang, 2 min — accumulating 60–90 s of hang across 2–3 sets, rest included
  • Optional (+8 min): couch stretch and pec holds, 2 min per side — only if you missed sauna sessions that week

The four segments add to 8 + 5 + 4 + 2 = 19 min as the core session, 27 with the optional one. This replaces the wellness tracker's "foam rolling & stretching, 10 min, 1×/week" — so it's +9 minutes a week, +36 per four weeks, and the cost table bills it at exactly that. Not a free restructure. It buys a defined session instead of a line item that, by your own account, was the first thing to evaporate.
