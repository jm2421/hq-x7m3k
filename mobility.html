Pre-lift routine
10 min · 11 on squat days · straight through
Lift days
  1. 0:00Hip CARs
    60 s1 circle each way / side
    Stand on one leg, core braced. Slow, maximal circles at the hip.
  2. 1:00Hip 90/90
    90 s2 s pause each end
    Flow side to side, torso tall.
  3. 2:30World's greatest stretch
    90 s3 / side
    Lunge, elbow inside the front foot, rotate and reach up. Then straighten the front leg for the hamstring.
  4. 4:00Quadruped T-spine rotation
    90 s5–6 / side
    Hand behind the head, elbow down to the opposite hand, then open to the ceiling. Eyes follow the elbow.
  5. 5:30Shoulder CARs
    90 smax circle, min speed
    Fist, arm forward and overhead, rotate outward, sweep behind and down.
  6. 7:00Scapular wall slides
    60 s8–10 reps
    Back to the wall, cactus arms, slide up — stop before the lumbar spine leaves the wall.
  7. 8:00Ankle dorsiflexion
    60 s120 s on squat days8–10 / side
    Face the wall, foot 3–4 in away, drive the knee over the toes, heel stays down. On squat days everything after this shifts a minute later.
  8. 9:00Dead hang
    60 s2 × 30 s
    Passive. Let the spine, lats and shoulders decompress. Breathe.

Three drills, five minutes, every lift day — Discover Strength or home. It is the highest-value five minutes in the program for long-term neck durability, and the first thing to get skipped.

N
Neck protocol
5 min · every lift day
Lift days
  1. 1Chin tucks
    2 × 105 s hold~2 min
    Deep neck flexors — what holds your head over your shoulders. No equipment, so worth doing daily rather than only on lift days.
  2. 2NeckSlevel
    2 × 10–12~2 minalternate direction
    Flexion/extension one session, lateral flexion both sides the next. Slow and controlled through a comfortable range — never ballistic, never into a pinch.
  3. 3Isometric holds
    3 directions × 10 s~1 min
    Also the fallback: on a stiff or symptomatic day do only these and skip the other two. A one-minute day beats a skipped one.
Nothing here should ever feel hard

Neck work earns its keep through consistency at submaximal effort, not through load. Pain, numbness, or symptoms running down an arm: stop for the day and take it to your clinician — that is a nerve-root question, not a training question.

Long holds go here rather than into the warm-up: the tissue is warm, you are not doing anything else, and accumulated time under tension is what moves range. Two or three of these per session, rotated so nothing gets skipped twice running.

F
Flexibility — in the sauna
6–12 min · ~3 exposures each per week
4× / week
  1. 1Couch stretch
    90–120 s / side
    Squeeze the glute and tuck the pelvis under before you lean back. Stop at the first sensation in the low back, not the hip.
  2. 2Pec stretch — doorway or floor
    90–120 s / side
    The direct counter to the week's pressing volume. Do this one early in the session, not at the end.
  3. 3Hamstring — seated or supine
    90–120 s / side
    Hinge from the hip, spine neutral. Don't round into it.
One caution on doing this in the heat

Long holds at 185 °F, especially standing ones, are a setup for lightheadedness — more so if hydration has been light, or if anything you take affects blood volume or appetite. Get water in beforehand, do the standing pec stretch early, and come out if you feel it. Heat also makes tissue feel more compliant than it is, so let range be governed by sensation rather than by how far you can get.

After the REHIT. Ninety seconds is the floor on any hold, not a target.

Sunday extended session

Weekly

    8 min2 + 3 + 3half pre-lift speed
    Hip CARs, shoulder CARs and quadruped T-spine rotation — the same three drills, slowed down. The other warm-up drills are not repeated here.

    Transitions first, then hold each end position.

    4 minextension holds
    Both hands behind the head so the neck never hinges backward. Hips down, ribs pulled in — the movement happens between the shoulder blades, not at the low back. Roller on the mid-back only, never the neck.

    2 min2–3 sets
    Accumulate 60–90 s of hanging, rest included.

    Only if you missed sauna sessions this week.

Notes

The warm-up is dynamic, the sauna is static. CARs and movement before lifting; save every long hold for the heat. They are different jobs and they don't substitute.
Ankle work doubles on squat days. Restricted dorsiflexion is often the real limiter behind restricted knee ROM and squat depth.
Thoracic extension is the highest-leverage segment on Sunday. A stiff mid-back is what pushes the neck and low back into compensating for it. That is the mechanism, not a measured result.
Plan the flexibility dose against four saunas a week. A fifth is a bonus, not part of the count.
